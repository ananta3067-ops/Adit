# Aastik Fertility: Market Sizing

**Rule used:** define the market as **couples Aastik can actually serve with a paid, non-IVF fertility programme**, not "India's $1 B IVF market" or "a $28 B fertility market". Investors discount top-down report numbers. They trust a funnel they can check.

## 1. Summary

| Layer | Definition | Couples / year | Value / year |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TAM** | Urban Indian couples having difficulty conceiving (or preparing for pregnancy) who pay for structured, non-IVF fertility care | **~9–10 lakh** | **₹3,000–7,000 Cr** |
| **SAM (Pune)** | Pune Metro: TTC 6–24 months with woman under 35, plus planners; affluent; open to Ayurveda or integrative care | **~3,000–4,200** | **₹17–28 Cr** |
| **SAM (expansion, top 8 metros)** | The same filter across Mumbai, Delhi NCR, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai, Kolkata, Ahmedabad and Pune | ~35,000–50,000 | ₹200–330 Cr |
| **SOM (3 years)** | What Katraj + 2 consult satellites can realistically win | **180–300** | **₹1.2–2.0 Cr** (5–8% of the Pune SAM) |

**What this tells an investor:** Pune alone supports a profitable clinic business of roughly ₹2–3 Cr. It does not support a venture-scale company. **Venture scale requires the multi-city SAM** (₹200–330 Cr), which is why the plan is a protocolised, repeatable site model with an outcomes registry. Gynoveda's ~₹68 Cr national revenue is about 1–2% of the TAM, consistent with this sizing.

## 4. SOM: what Aastik can win in 3 years

| Year | Sites | New couples/year | Share of Pune SAM | Programme revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Y1 (pilot) | Katraj | ~63 | ~2% | ~₹43 L |
| Y2 | Katraj + Baner consult satellite | ~150–180 | ~4–5% | ~₹1.0–1.2 Cr |
| Y3 | + Wakad/Kharadi satellite | ~180–300 | ~5–8% | **~₹1.2–2.0 Cr** (plus OPD and medicine repeats) |

**The capacity limit:** about 110–120 active couples per 2 therapy rooms and 1 doctor (Venture Lab 6.4). Y3 requires a second doctor.

## 5. How to make these numbers investor-grade (next 6 weeks)

1. Replace the **affluence** and **openness to Ayurveda** assumptions with data from the 30 couple interviews and the deposit test.
2. Ask the 8 gynaecologists you interview: "How many couples trying to conceive under age 35 do you see a month, and what share ask about Ayurveda?" Then multiply by the number of OBGs in Pune's affluent belts. That gives an independent estimate of segment B.
3. Get the real Pune birth count from the PMC/PCMC civil registration reports.
4. Show the SAM as a range on slide 8, with this funnel in the appendix.

**Unverified inputs:** the national birth count, the Pune birth rate, the Pune affluence share, the share open to Ayurveda, and the 6-month non-conception rate (roughly 20% from standard fecundability data). Treat the outputs as ±30%.
