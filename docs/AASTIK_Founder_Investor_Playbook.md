## 1. The hypothesis (made explicit so every test traces back to it)

**Product working name:** `AASTIK-HRD-01` (Haridra platform). A trade name needs a trademark search first, because Curcuwin, CurQfen, BCM-95, Meriva, Longvida and Theracurmin are all taken.

**H1 — Bioavailability (PK) hypothesis**
> A single oral dose of AASTIK-HRD-01 (standardised turmeric CO₂/solvent extract, curcuminoids + turmerones, delivered as an enteric-protected pellet inside a capsule, CIC) produces **≥5-fold higher AUC₀–t of *free* (unconjugated) curcumin** and a higher Cmax than an equal-curcuminoid dose of a **standard 95% curcuminoid extract**, in healthy adults under fasting conditions.

**H2 — Clinical efficacy hypothesis**
> In adults with knee osteoarthritis (Kellgren-Lawrence grade II–III), AASTIK-HRD-01 for 12 weeks reduces **WOMAC total score** and **VAS pain** significantly more than placebo. It also lowers **hs-CRP / IL-6**, with safety comparable to placebo.

**H3 — Real-world (commercial) hypothesis**
> When prescribed through Aastik's doctor network with CRM follow-up, **≥60% of patients refill at day 30** and doctors report a meaningful improvement in pain and function. This is tracked in AASTIK TECH™.

**Why knee OA / joint pain?** (CEO logic)
1. It links to your existing hero SKU **Vajraortho** and to your **Pune pain-management clinic**, which can act as a real-world evidence site.
2. The curcumin evidence base is strongest in OA, so a positive result is more likely.
3. It is a large, chronic, repeat-purchase category with high willingness to pay.
4. It works in all three channels: Ayurveda Rx (*Sandhigata Vata*), FSSAI ("supports joint comfort/mobility") and export.

**CHALLENGE:** "Bioavailable curcumin" is a crowded race. Arjuna (BCM-95), OmniActive (Curcuwin), Akay (CurQfen), Sami-Sabinsa and Indena (Meriva) all have PK papers. Why will you win?
**ANSWER:** You will not win on "X-fold bioavailability" alone. You win on **(a) the Ayurveda-doctor channel those ingredient companies do not own**, **(b) outcome data from your own RCT plus CRM real-world data**, **(c) price**, and **(d) a platform** (CIC/EC pellets) that you can reuse for Ashwagandha, Boswellia, Guggulu and others. Curcumin is the **proof case for the platform**, not the whole company.

## 3. PK (pharmacokinetic) data: tests, design, time and cost

### 3.1 Study design (industry-standard; mirrors published Indian curcumin PK studies registered on CTRI)
| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| Title | Randomised, double-blind, single-dose, 3-treatment, 3-period, 6-sequence crossover comparative bioavailability study of AASTIK-HRD-01 vs standard 95% curcuminoid extract in healthy adults under fasting conditions |
| Arms | **T1:** AASTIK-HRD-01 (low dose, e.g. 250 mg curcuminoids) · **T2:** AASTIK-HRD-01 (equal dose to reference) · **R:** 95% curcuminoid extract (e.g. 1,000–1,500 mg) |
| Subjects | **18–24 healthy adults** (18–45 y, BMI 18.5–30), plus 10–15% over-enrolment for dropouts |
| Washout | 7 days between periods |
| Sampling | Pre-dose and **12–15 time points over 24 h** (0.25, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 24 h); optional 48 h |
| Diet control | Curcumin/turmeric-free diet 72 h before each period; standardised meals |
| Bioanalysis | **Validated LC-MS/MS** (ICH M10 bioanalytical guideline), **LLOQ ≤ 0.5–1 ng/mL** |
| Analytes | **Free (unconjugated) curcumin = primary.** Secondary: DMC, BDMC, **total curcumin after β-glucuronidase/sulfatase hydrolysis**, tetrahydrocurcumin |
| Safety | Vitals, AEs, baseline and end-of-study CBC/LFT/RFT |
| Registration | IEC approval → **CTRI registration before first subject** → GCP conduct |

**Credibility tip (investor-grade):** many curcumin brands report only "total curcuminoids after deconjugation", which inflates fold-increase claims. **Report free curcumin as primary.** Scientific advisors on investor panels will notice and respect this.

### 3.2 PK parameters you will report
| Parameter | Meaning | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Cmax** | Peak plasma concentration | Whether enough curcumin reaches blood to act |
| **Tmax** | Time to peak | Proves enteric/sustained delivery (should shift later and be broader) |
| **AUC₀–t / AUC₀–∞** | Total exposure over time | **Basis of the "X-times bioavailability" claim** |
| **t½** | Elimination half-life | Supports once/twice-daily dosing |
| F-rel (derived) | (AUC_T/AUC_R) × (Dose_R/Dose_T) | The headline number for marketing (dose-normalised fold increase) |
| Also | Kel, MRT, Cmax/AUC ratio | Completeness for publication |

## 4. Human efficacy trial: cost and what you need

### 4.1 Recommended design
| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| Design | Multicentre, randomised, **double-blind, placebo-controlled**, parallel, 2 arms (optional 3rd arm: 95% extract to prove superiority) |

## 5. CDSCO vs FSSAI (and the third option most founders miss)

### 5.1 The three real routes for a curcumin product in India
| | **AYUSH: Ayurvedic Proprietary Medicine** | **FSSAI: Nutraceutical / Health Supplement** | **CDSCO: Phytopharmaceutical / New Drug** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Law | Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940 + Rules (Rule 158-B), ASU licensing | FSS Act 2006 → **Nutraceutical Regulations 2022** + **Advertising & Claims Regulations 2018** | New Drugs & Clinical Trials Rules 2019 (phytopharmaceutical drug definition) |
| Licensing body | **State AYUSH Drug Licensing Authority** (this is how your 9 SKUs are licensed) | FSSAI central licence (manufacturer/marketer) | CDSCO (DCGI) |
| What you may say | **Indication** in Ayurvedic terms (e.g. *"Useful in Sandhigata Vata / joint pain"*) | **Structure/function claims only** ("supports joint comfort & mobility"); **no** disease claims; label must say *"NOT FOR MEDICINAL USE"* | Full disease indication ("treatment of knee OA") |
| Evidence needed | Safety + effectiveness per Rule 158-B (textual rationale + published literature; pilot study for proprietary formulations) | Ingredient within Schedule limits; **product-specific claims need prior approval with human data** | Full preclinical + **Phase I–III** clinical |
| Time | 2–4 months | 1–3 months (licence); 3–9 months (claim approval) | **5–8 years** |
| Cost | ₹1–3 L | ₹1–3 L licence; ₹2–5 L claim dossier (excluding studies) | **₹20–60 Cr+** |
| Channel fit | **Doctor Rx (your core)**, AYUSH OPDs, institutions, GeM | D2C, Amazon, quick commerce, pharmacy OTC, gyms, export (as supplement) | Allopathic doctors, hospital formularies |
| Advertising limits | Drugs & Magic Remedies Act; Rule 170 (status has been in litigation since 2024, so check the current position) | Claims Regulations 2018; misleading-ad penalties | Rx-only promotion rules (UCPMP) |

### 5.2 Recommendation (CEO + COO)
**AYUSH first, FSSAI second, CDSCO only via a partner later.**

1. **Now:** license `AASTIK-HRD-01` as an **Ayurvedic Proprietary Medicine** with your state licensing authority. This keeps your doctor channel, allows indication language and is cheap. The PK + RCT data then becomes **marketing evidence for doctors** and makes you stand out on every MR visit.
2. **In parallel (month 6–12):** launch a **sister SKU under FSSAI** (same core technology, different brand/pack) for D2C, pharmacy and export, and **apply for product-specific claim approval** once the PK paper is out.
3. **CDSCO later, and not on your own balance sheet.** After the RCT, the phytopharmaceutical route can be **licensed to an Indian pharma partner** (upfront + milestones + royalty). This is a B2B revenue line, not a cost line.

**CHALLENGE:** "Investors say FSSAI nutraceuticals scale faster than Ayurveda Rx. Why not go FSSAI-only?"
**ANSWER:** FSSAI-only means competing on Instagram ad spend against heavily funded D2C brands with no unfair advantage. Your **unfair advantage is doctor trust + alumni access**, which only the AYUSH route fully monetises. Running both routes from one technology costs little extra. That **dual-licence architecture is itself a moat.**

**CHALLENGE 2:** "Why did you say CDSCO in the brief?"
**ANSWER:** Ayurvedic drugs are regulated under the D&C Act through the AYUSH vertical and state licensing authorities. CDSCO matters only for (a) a phytopharmaceutical/new-drug filing and (b) registered BA/BE centres and ethics committees. **Use a CDSCO-registered BA/BE centre for your PK study** anyway, because it raises credibility at no regulatory cost.

### 5.3 The "4 parameters" for curcumin
**If you take the drug-style route (AYUSH/CDSCO), measure these 4 in the PK study:**
1. **Cmax**: peak free-curcumin concentration
2. **Tmax**: time to peak
3. **AUC₀–t (and AUC₀–∞)**: total exposure, which is the bioavailability claim
4. **t½**: elimination half-life (dosing frequency)

**And these 4 clinical/biomarker parameters in the RCT:**
1. **WOMAC** (pain, stiffness, function), the primary endpoint
2. **VAS pain** (0–100 mm)
3. **hs-CRP** (systemic inflammation)
4. **IL-6** (or TNF-α), the mechanism link to curcumin's NF-κB pathway

### 5.4 If FSSAI: which claims are permitted, and how to earn more
**Generally permissible (structure/function, if substantiated and within Schedule usage limits):**
- "Supports joint comfort and mobility"
- "Antioxidant support"
- "Supports a healthy inflammatory response"
- "Supports digestive health" (turmeric, traditional use)

**Never permitted on a food/nutraceutical label:** "treats/cures/prevents arthritis", "anti-cancer", "replaces painkillers", or any disease name used as a treatment claim.

**Claims you can *earn* with your own data (need FSSAI prior approval under Claims Regs 2018 / nutraceutical rules):**
| Claim | Evidence you submit |
|---|---|
| "**X× better absorbed** than standard 95% curcumin extract" (comparative) | Your **published PK study** (Part 3); name the comparator and dose |
| "**Clinically shown** to improve joint comfort in 8–12 weeks" | Your **published RCT** (Part 4) |
| "Reduces joint discomfort after exercise" (sports positioning) | Small RCT in active adults (n ≈ 60) |

**How to file (step by step):**

## 7. How we disrupt the market

**The gap:** Indian Ayurveda splits into (a) **classical/proprietary pharmacies**, which doctors trust but which have no modern data and old formats, and (b) **D2C/nutra brands**, which have marketing and some data but which Ayurveda doctors do not trust. **No one owns "doctor-trusted + data-proven + modern delivery."**

**Aastik's disruption, in five moves:**
1. **"Evidence per SKU."** Every hero product ships with its own PK/RCT summary card for doctors. Competitors cite generic turmeric literature; you cite *your* product's study.
2. **"Scan the batch."** A QR code on every strip shows the COA, HPLC fingerprint, heavy-metal and pesticide results. This addresses the main trust problem in Ayurveda (contamination and inconsistency).
3. **Price disruption.** Bioavailable curcumins retail around ₹25–60/day. Target **₹12–18/day** on doctor channel MRP with 60%+ gross margin, because you are a lean formulator, not an ingredient multinational.
4. **Outcome-linked prescribing.** The CRM tracks patient VAS/WOMAC-lite at day 0/15/30, so each doctor sees *their own* patients' outcomes. Doctors keep prescribing what they can see working, and this drives retention.
5. **Alumni + CME + clinic flywheel.** The alumni network gives access, CMEs give authority, the clinic gives real-world proof, and AI-MR gives speed. Each new institution adds new alumni advocates.

## 10. Red-team loop: Ask → Answer → Challenge → Final answer

*(This is the self-questioning loop you asked for, compressed to the questions investors will actually ask.)*

**Q1. "Your deck says '9 FDA-approved formulations'. What does that mean?"**
- *Answer:* State licensing authority approvals.
- *Challenge:* In India "FDA approved" usually refers to state FDA (Food & Drug Administration) licensing, but investors read "FDA" as the US FDA. Any diligence will flag this as overclaiming.
- **Final:** Change it to **"9 products licensed under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act by the State AYUSH Licensing Authority."** Accurate wording builds trust.

**Q2. "You're 'MD (Final Year)'. Are you qualified?"**
- **Final:** Update the deck. You are **MD Ayurveda in Rasashastra & Bhaishajya Kalpana**, the pharmaceutics specialisation of Ayurveda. That is exactly the expertise for a formulation-and-delivery company. Lead with the thesis work (HPTLC/LC-MS standardisation, cell-line work).

**Q3. "Women-led majority shareholding, but you're the decision-maker. Is this a grant-eligibility structure?"**
- **Final:** Give the majority shareholder a real operating role, a board seat and vesting, and put it in the SHA. Otherwise present it neutrally and don't lead with it.

**Q4. "LTV:CAC 8–28× and payback under 1 month? That's not believable."**
- *Challenge:* Numbers that look too good reduce credibility.
- **Final:** Show **actual cohort data** from the CRM (prescribers by month, 3/6/9-month retention, revenue per doctor). Present a conservative **4–6× LTV:CAC and 3–5 month payback**, then beat it.

**Q5. "TAM ₹5 lakh crore?"**
- **Final:** Use bottom-up sizing. Example: *7+ lakh AYUSH practitioners × 10% addressable in 5 states × ₹2.4 L/yr potential per active prescriber.* Add the joint-health supplement segment and B2B ingredient licensing. A top-down TAM gets a polite nod; a bottom-up SOM gets a term sheet.

**Q6. "Why will curcumin work when 20 companies already sell bioavailable curcumin?"**
- **Final:** Curcumin is the **proof case for a reusable delivery platform**, sold through a channel competitors don't own, with outcome data competitors don't have. The platform slide shows 5 botanicals on the same technology.

**Q7. "What's patentable? Indian law excludes traditional knowledge (Sec 3(p)) and mere admixtures (Sec 3(e))."**
- **Final:** Patent the **delivery architecture and process** (pellet composition, coating ratio, CIC dual-phase release profile, a specific dissolution signature), and show **synergy data** (the PK study) to overcome 3(e). File provisionally before any publication or conference talk.

**Q8. "You don't own a factory. What stops your CMO from copying you?"**
- **Final:** Use an NDA + IP-assignment clause in the manufacturing agreement, split the process across two vendors (pellet coating vs filling), own the specs, and keep a second CMO validated. Asset-light is a strength until ₹75–100 Cr revenue.

**Q9. "Rajasthan focus, but your clinic is in Pune. Which is it?"**
- **Final:** **Pune = R&D, clinical and HQ hub** (incubator, CROs, labs, clinic RWE). **Rajasthan + Karnataka + Maharashtra = commercial launch states**, chosen for alumni density. Say this clearly on one slide.

**Q10. "What happens if the RCT fails?"**
- **Final:** You still have a licensed Rx business with 9 SKUs, a PK-proven HRD-01 (FSSAI comparative absorption claim), and a platform. Bear case: a ₹150–250 Cr business. Downside protected, upside open.

**Q11. "Why should a VC care about a doctor-led channel when D2C scales faster?"**
- **Final:** D2C acquisition costs in Indian wellness have been rising, and returning customers get expensive to win back. A doctor prescription brings repeat purchase on a 30–45-day cycle at near-zero marginal CAC once the doctor is active. You add D2C on top in FY28 with approved claims, so it is evidence-led rather than ad-led.

**Q12. "Ask: ₹35–45 L for what proof point?"**
- **Final:** Reframe as a **₹1–1.3 Cr Round 0 (60–70% non-dilutive)** that buys **three proofs in 12 months**: (1) human PK data, (2) patent filed, (3) prescriber cohort retention. Each one moves valuation.
