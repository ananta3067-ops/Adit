## 3. The problem and the science

**Problem**
- Curcumin is one of the most-researched plant compounds (23,000+ publications in 2014–2023 per Web of Science analysis), but oral bioavailability is **under 1%**. It is poorly soluble, rapidly metabolised and mostly eliminated in bile and faeces (Anand et al., 2007; Hewlings & Kalman, 2017).
- Doctors see inconsistent results, so medical adoption is limited.

**What published evidence shows**
- **Shoba et al., 1998:** 20 mg piperine + 2 g curcumin raised serum curcumin about 2,000% (~20×) in 10 healthy men, with both given **together**.
- **Zeng et al., 2017 (rats):** piperine given 0.5–8 h **before** curcumin raised exposure up to ~6×; given together at 20:1, only ~1.7×.
- **Volak et al., 2008 (in vitro, human enzymes):** piperine is a selective CYP3A inhibitor.
- **Human evidence for timing/sequencing does not exist yet.** That gap is our opportunity.

**Our hypothesis (untested in humans)**
Releasing piperine ahead of curcumin changes how the intestine handles curcumin, giving higher and more consistent exposure than simultaneous dosing.

**CiC 2.0 design**
- **Outer capsule:** fast-release BioPerine® (≥95% piperine), ~5 mg: the "primer"
- **Time gap:** target 15–20 min, set by inner-capsule dissolution and gastric emptying (to be validated)
- **Inner capsule:** Curcumin C3 Complex® (≥95% curcuminoids), ~250 mg: the "payload"
- Made on **existing dual-capsule manufacturing technology**, with no exotic equipment. The novelty is the formulation, timing protocol and patent.
- **Dose:** 1 capsule twice daily; course 60 days = 12 strips (10 capsules each)

**Important honesty point:** the 20× figure is from a different dose (2 g + 20 mg). We do **not** claim it applies to our 250 mg + 5 mg dose. Our study tests our own dose.

## 4. The PK study (what Tranche 1 pays for)

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Design | Randomised, 3-arm crossover in healthy volunteers |
| Arm 1 | Curcumin alone |
| Arm 2 | Curcumin + piperine simultaneous (single capsule), the industry standard |

| Publication | **Published whatever the result**, positive or negative |

**Why healthy volunteers?** The question is simply "does more curcumin reach the blood?" Healthy volunteers answer it faster, cheaper and more cleanly. Patient outcome studies come later.

### The pass mark (the "gate"), fixed in advance
All must hold, signed off by an independent biostatistician:
1. CiC AUC **≥ 1.25× the simultaneous arm**, with the **lower 90% confidence bound above 1.0**
2. No product-related serious adverse event
3. Ethics approval and CTRI registration in place before the first volunteer was dosed
4. FSSAI licence in hand and the first commercial batch released with a COA
5. Monthly MIS sent to investors M1–M8, with spend within 15% of budget

**Why 1.25×?** 0.80–1.25 is the standard range regulators use to call two products "equivalent". Above 1.25×, with the lower bound above 1.0, means CiC is genuinely **better** than taking both together, not just the same.

## 5. Regulatory route

- **CiC 2.0 → FSSAI nutraceutical** (Nutraceutical Regulations 2022 + Advertising & Claims Regulations 2018).
- **Why FSSAI and not Ayurvedic or phytopharmaceutical?** Allopathic doctors can't prescribe Ayurvedic medicines. The phytopharmaceutical (CDSCO drug) route needs much longer and costlier trials (years, tens of crores). FSSAI is the fastest route to market; the trade-off is **no disease claims**. A later product can take a drug route if data supports it.
- **Claims:** only FSSAI-permitted structure/function wording now. An absorption claim comes **only after** the PK study is published and FSSAI approves it.
- **Doctor engagement policy:** no gifts or cash to doctors, no volume discounts to prescribers, CMEs with real educational content only, nothing beyond what FSSAI allows (UCPMP / NMC / NCISM-compliant).
- **Do not call CiC 2.0 a "phytopharmaceutical"** or a drug.
- **GST:** modelled at **18%** (cautious). Published sources suggest **5%** for nutraceuticals (HSN 2106); CA confirmation PENDING. At 5%, net per strip rises from ₹286 to about ₹322.

## 17. Glossary (for quick reference)

- **PK (pharmacokinetics):** how much of a compound reaches the blood, and how fast.
- **AUC:** area under the blood-concentration curve, i.e. total exposure. **Cmax:** peak level. **Tmax:** time to peak.
- **Crossover study:** each volunteer takes every version, in turn, with a washout gap.
- **CTRI:** Clinical Trials Registry – India (mandatory registration).
- **FSSAI:** food & nutraceutical regulator. **AYUSH licence:** for Ayurvedic medicines. **CDSCO:** drug regulator.
- **CiC:** capsule-in-capsule. **MR:** medical representative. **CME:** continuing medical education session.
- **Pre-money / post-money:** valuation before / after the new money. **Tranche:** money released in parts on milestones.
- **1× non-participating liquidation preference:** standard, founder-friendly. **Broad-based weighted-average anti-dilution:** fair; refuse "full ratchet".
